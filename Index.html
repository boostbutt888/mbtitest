Learn about this personality test

This is a 32-question educational self-reflection exercise inspired by the four preference pairs used in the Myers–Briggs framework. Each question asks how much a statement matches your usual preferences, using five response choices. Eight statements explore each preference pair. Your answers are combined into a four-letter pattern, such as INFP or ESTJ.

The four preference pairs explained
PairWhat it explores
E / IExtraversion: engagement with people and the external world. Introversion: reflection and the inner world. Neither means social skill or shyness.
S / NSensing: details, evidence and present realities. Intuition: patterns, interpretations and possibilities. Both are useful ways to notice information.
T / FThinking: prioritizing impersonal logic and consistency. Feeling: prioritizing values and effects on people. Both use thought and emotion.
J / PJudging: preferring plans, structure and closure. Perceiving: preferring adaptability, exploration and openness. Neither is about being judgmental or perceptive.
How scoring and results work

The test balances statements written in both directions for each preference pair. Answers are scored from strongly disagree to strongly agree; a neutral response contributes no preference. The more your answers lean in one direction, the greater the displayed percentage for that preference. A tie is assigned to the first letter as a technical tie-breaker, so near-50/50 results should be treated as undecided.

These percentages describe the answers to this particular questionnaire. They are not scientific confidence levels, fixed personality strengths, or official MBTI scores. A person's behavior can vary by context, experience and time. If your result feels wrong, explore adjacent types and decide which description fits best.

What this assessment can and cannot tell you

Useful for: reflecting on communication, teamwork, learning habits, decision-making and preferred working styles.

Not suitable for: hiring, employee ranking, diagnosing conditions, predicting performance, or determining someone's abilities. This original test has not undergone reliability, validity or norming studies and is not the official MBTI® assessment.
